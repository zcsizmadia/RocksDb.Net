# RocksDb 11.8.1 — i7-12800H

```text
BenchmarkDotNet v0.15.4, Windows 11 (10.0.26200.9168)
12th Gen Intel Core i7-12800H 2.40GHz, 1 CPU, 20 logical and 14 physical cores
.NET SDK 10.0.400
Job=ShortRun  IterationCount=3  WarmupCount=3
```

**These are ShortRun numbers.** Three iterations, so the timing error bars are
wide and small differences in the Mean column mean nothing. Read them for order
of magnitude and for the allocation columns, which BenchmarkDotNet measures
exactly rather than statistically and which are therefore the trustworthy part
of this file. A full run is needed before quoting any timing.

## Callbacks

What a managed comparator costs against RocksDb doing the same comparison
natively. Both implement bytewise ordering, so the difference is the boundary
and not the algorithm.

| Method | Keys | Lookups | Mean | Ratio | Allocated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RocksDb's own comparator | 100,000 | 1,000 | 1.215 ms | 1.00 | 101.56 KB |
| Managed comparator | 100,000 | 1,000 | 1.288 ms | 1.06 | 101.56 KB |

**About 6%, and no extra allocation.** Each lookup binary-searches within
blocks, so a thousand lookups is tens of thousands of boundary crossings —
which puts the per-crossing cost very low. This is the number #148 was argued
without.

## Event listeners

Whether a notification a listener does not want is expensive. Directly the
question in #153, which asks if the reflection that gates the ten event slots
could be dropped in favour of always notifying.

| Method | Flushes | Mean | Ratio | Allocated | Alloc Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No listener attached | 20 | 12.54 ms | 1.00 | 173.16 KB | 1.00 |
| Listener overriding nothing | 20 | 11.40 ms | 0.91 | 173.32 KB | 1.00 |
| Listener overriding five events | 20 | 12.14 ms | 0.97 | 355.60 KB | 2.05 |

The timings are noise — the error bars are wider than the differences, because
each invocation opens a database and flushes twenty times. Ignore them.

The allocations are not noise, and they answer the question in a way the
timings could not:

- A listener that overrides nothing allocates **the same** as no listener at
  all. The boundary crossing itself is free.
- A listener that overrides five events allocates **twice** as much. That is the
  cost of marshalling the job-info objects the events carry.

So the managed-side gate is earning its keep on **allocation**, not on
transitions: what it avoids is constructing info objects nobody will read.
"Always notify and let an un-overridden virtual return" would impose that cost
on every listener, unless info construction were made lazy as well — which is a
larger change than the one that ticket proposes.

That argues for the option where the subclass declares which events it wants,
keeping the saving without the reflection, rather than for dropping the gate.
